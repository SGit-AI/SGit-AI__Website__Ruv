How it works

From a first conversation to a pilot and a setup you run yourself: who does what, how a provider is matched, the provider workflow from enquiry to handover, how each job is delivered as a ruv.sgit run, and how what we learn goes back to the public repositories.

From article No. 9, From the talk to the service, with the decisions of 21 October applied (enquire to book). The money and ownership rules are on Rules of engagement. Built on rUv's open-source projects (Ruflo, RuVector, RVM, MetaHarness): MIT, by rUv / ruvnet.

How a provider is matched

For now, by hand. An order or an enquiry arrives at agent@riskmandate.ai (a live agent answers; a person follows up). If it names a preferred provider, it goes to them. If not, the founders match it by project type, language, location and availability, and reply with one or two names. The client chooses. Nothing is automated.

A client can name a provider from the start: each provider's page has its own enquire to book button, and the shop has a preferred-provider field (with Dinis Cruz, with Mondweep Chakravorty). See the providers.

The provider workflow, from enquiry to handover

What a founding provider does with an engagement, step by step: who does it, what it produces, and where that lives. Client material lives in email and in the engagement's private vault, and never in this site's git repository. The only step that touches a public repository is step 8, and it carries no client name or data.

  1. An enquiry arrives

    The client writes to agent@riskmandate.ai, from an enquire to book button on this site or from the shop, naming a preferred provider or none. The enquiry says who it is for, the offer, what agents should do, what they must never do, and when to start. A live agent answers; a person follows up.

    Who
    The client; the agent at agent@riskmandate.ai acknowledges
    Produces
    The enquiry email (or the shop's emailed order)
    Lives in
    email the mailbox at agent@riskmandate.ai; never this git repository

  2. Triage and match

    By hand, by the two founders. A named provider gets the enquiry. Otherwise the founders match by project type, language, location and availability, and reply with one or two names; the client chooses. The time to reply is agreed with the client. From here the provider who has the client is the client's single contact.

    Who
    The two founders
    Produces
    A reply to the client naming the provider (or two to choose from)
    Lives in
    email

  3. The first conversation (free)

    The provider who has the client talks it through: the objective, what must never happen, the materials, and how sensitive the data is. It ends with a proposed first run.

    Who
    The provider who has the client, with the client (and the delivering provider, if another)
    Produces
    Notes, and a proposed first run, sent to the client
    Lives in
    email between the client and the provider; the client's materials wait for the run's private vault

  4. The quote, and the written note between providers

    Whoever has the client bills, quotes and signs with the client: list prices, GBP excluding VAT, direct costs itemised. When more than one provider is involved, they agree a short written note (an email is enough to start): who bills, who delivers, who the parties are, and that revenue is split equally after direct costs.

    Who
    The provider who has the client; every provider in the engagement for the note
    Produces
    The quote; the note between providers; the contract, signed by whoever bills
    Lives in
    email between the parties

  5. The run spec, and a mandate per role

    The delivering provider writes the run spec (the client's objective, the materials, one task per worker) and an Agent Behaviour Policy for each role: what it can reach, what it may do, and what is stopped. No network by default.

    Who
    The provider(s) who deliver; the client agrees the objective and the limits
    Produces
    The run spec and one mandate (ABP) per role
    Lives in
    private vault the engagement's vault; never this git repository (the public run specs here are for our own runs)

  6. The run: open, deliver, close

    The vault is created when the run opens, with a branch per worker; every delivery is committed and merged, and the server stores ciphertext only. A provider starts the run and watches it to the end. At the close the vault is measured (commits, files, bytes, a plaintext probe of the server's store, a fresh clone compared file by file), then handed to the client as a zip or with a read key they choose to publish, and deleted from our side with the delete checked, or sealed for the client.

    Who
    The provider(s) who deliver; the agents (a Ruflo queen and workers) under their mandates
    Produces
    The deliveries, the measurements, the vault for the client
    Lives in
    private vault until it is handed over and deleted from our side

  7. Review and handover; the invoice

    The delivering provider reviews what the run made and hands it over with the vault. Whoever has the client invoices: direct costs are reimbursed first to whoever paid them, itemised; what is left is split equally, and the biller pays the other parties their share. The biller charges VAT where it applies.

    Who
    The delivering provider (review and handover); the provider who has the client (the invoice and the payments)
    Produces
    The handover to the client; the invoice; the shares paid
    Lives in
    private vault with the client; the invoice by email

  8. Commit back

    Everything that is not the client's (a better playbook, a fix to the runner, a new behaviour-policy pattern, a reusable worker role) goes back to the public repositories as a pull request, with the client's name and data removed, credited to the provider who made it. Improvements to rUv's projects are offered to them under their own licence and contribution rules.

    Who
    The provider who made the change
    Produces
    Pull requests to the public repositories
    Lives in
    public repositories code and playbooks only; no client name, code or data

  9. The ledger entry

    What ran, how long, and what the checks found. Private by default for client work; published only with the client's written agreement, and then only the measurements, never the content.

    Who
    The delivering provider
    Produces
    The ledger entry for the run
    Lives in
    private record kept with the engagement; on the public ledger only with the client's agreement

Nothing here is automated: a person matches, starts, watches and hands over every run. The rules behind each step are on Rules of engagement (rules 4 to 9, and 11 to 16).

How the work is delivered

Every job is a ruv.sgit run, the same way every material on this site was made (see one run, six steps):

  1. A run spec

    The client's objective, the materials, and one task per worker.

  2. A mandate per role

    An Agent Behaviour Policy for each worker: what it can reach, what it may do, and what is stopped. No network by default.

  3. A vault per run

    Created when the run opens, a branch per worker, every delivery committed and merged. The server stores ciphertext only.

  4. A person watches

    A provider starts the run, watches it to the end and reviews what it made.

  5. The close

    The vault is measured (commits, files, bytes, a plaintext probe of the server's store, a fresh clone compared file by file), handed to the client as a zip or with a read key they choose to publish, and deleted from our side, with the delete checked.

  6. A ledger entry

    What ran, how long, and what the checks found. Private by default for client work; public with the client's agreement.

How changes are committed back

During a job, two kinds of change happen. The client's own code and data go into the client's vault and stay there (rule 4). Everything else, a better playbook, a fix to the runner, a new behaviour-policy pattern, a reusable worker role, goes back to the public repositories as a pull request, with the client's name and data removed, and is credited to the provider who made it (rule 5). The changes list of each public repository then shows what the engagements taught us.
