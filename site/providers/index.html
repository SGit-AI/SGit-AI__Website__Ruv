Why join

"My client wants agents doing the work. They also want to know what the agents were allowed to do, what they actually did, and where the data went. I cannot spend three weeks wiring that up for every engagement."

A method you do not have to build

Every job is a ruv.sgit run: a Ruflo queen and workers, a written mandate per role (an Agent Behaviour Policy), and an encrypted sgit vault per run that holds every brief, delivery and decision. The playbooks and the runner are published.

Clear terms about money and ownership

Published list prices, the same for every provider. Revenue split equally between the parties after direct costs. Whoever has the client bills. The client's code and data stay the client's.

Clients, and a fair way in

Enquiries and orders arrive at one address and are matched by hand to the provider who fits. You join through six published steps, and you are listed only with your consent.

Evidence your clients can check

Each run closes measured, and the client keeps the vault. Every run that made this site is in the ledger, and POC1 shows Ruflo's policy engine enforcing a behaviour policy.

1 · DO IT YOURSELF

Copy everything

The rules of engagement, in brief

  1. Everything here is open. Our code is Apache-2.0, our materials CC BY 4.0; rUv's projects are MIT, by rUv / ruvnet.
  2. You can do this without us, and we encourage it: no permission, no fee, no need to tell us.
  3. Code created in engagements through us is open, published under Apache-2.0.
  4. The client's code and data are the client's. Never published, reused or kept; each job runs in its own vault, handed to the client.
  5. Changes are committed back to the public repositories, with the client's name and data removed.
  6. Revenue is split equally, after costs. Direct costs go back to whoever paid them; the rest is split equally. No platform share.
  7. Whoever has the client bills, and pays the other parties their share.
  8. Custom arrangements are possible, agreed in writing before the work starts.
  9. Every job leaves evidence: a vault per run, a mandate per role, a ledger entry, private unless the client agrees.
  10. Credit where it is due: rUv's projects are named and credited as his.

How a provider is matched

For now, by hand. An order or an enquiry arrives at agent@riskmandate.ai: a live agent answers and a person follows up. If it names a preferred provider, it goes to them. If not, the founders match it by project type, language, location and availability, and reply with one or two names. The client chooses. Nothing is automated.

The provider who has the client scopes the job, writes the brief with the client, bills, and is the client's single contact.

How the work is delivered

  1. A run spec and a mandate per role

    The client's objective, the materials, a task per worker, and a behaviour policy for each: what it can reach, what it may do, what is stopped. No network by default.

  2. A vault per run, and a person watching

    Every delivery committed to an encrypted vault; you start the run, watch it to the end and review what it made.

  3. The close

    The vault is measured, handed to the client, and deleted from our side. Improvements to the shared code and playbooks go back as pull requests, credited to you.

How to join: six steps, from applying to being listed

Applications go on the waiting list. Until there are at least five providers, the founders do the proof-of-work review and the interview themselves; after that, peer review is by two members.

  1. Apply

    A short form that writes an email to agent@riskmandate.ai: what you deliver, which offers, your languages and links. Applications are kept privately, never in this repository.

  2. Show proof of work

    A public vault or repository of a real run you delivered.

  3. Peer review

    Providers already listed look at your proof of work.

  1. An interview

    A conversation about how you work, your clients and the rules.

  2. A shadowed first pilot

    Your first engagement, delivered alongside a listed provider.

  3. Standards, and listed

    You sign up to the standards and the rules of engagement, tick consent, and your profile goes on this page.
